Debt priority ranking

Pay all required minimums first. Rank balances by your preferred repayment method; urgency bands are illustrative and do not override past-due bills, essential expenses or loan protections.

01 / Stabilize

Build a cash safety net

  • Keep essentials and required debt payments current.
  • Maintain a starter emergency fund before aggressive payoff; build toward 3–6 months of essential expenses as circumstances allow.
  • Capture an affordable employer retirement match if available; consider vesting, plan terms and urgent obligations.
02 / Choose a method

Avalanche vs. snowball

  • Avalanche: Pay all minimums, target the highest APR with extra cash, then repeat. Usually reduces interest most.
  • Snowball: Pay all minimums, target the smallest current balance, then repeat. Early wins may help motivation.
  • Automate minimum payments and track each payoff; roll freed payments forward.
CFPB debt reduction guidance ↗
03 / Prioritize

Debt vs. investing

  • High APR (roughly 10%+): Often prioritize repayment over unmatched taxable investing.
  • Mid APR (roughly 5–10%): Consider a mix of payoff and investing based on cash needs and risk.
  • Low fixed APR (roughly under 5%): Investing alongside scheduled payments may be reasonable with adequate reserves and a long horizon.

These thresholds are illustrative, not rules. Paying down debt avoids interest at the contractual rate; market returns are uncertain. Taxes, deductions, loan protections and liquidity matter.

04 / Execute

Reduce interest and avoid setbacks

  • Ask lenders about lower rates or hardship options before missed payments.
  • Evaluate refinance or balance transfers including fees, promotional expiration and total interest.
  • For a mortgage, verify extra funds are applied to principal and check prepayment terms.
  • For student loans, check forgiveness and other borrower protections before refinancing or paying early.

05 / Compare opportunity cost

Extra payment vs. market assumption

This compares a hypothetical debt cost compounded monthly with an investment growing at an effective annual rate. It assumes the debt remains outstanding over the full horizon and does not calculate actual amortization savings, taxes or risk.

06 / Redirect

After paying off debt

  • Redirect the previous payment into reserves, retirement accounts or a diversified investment portfolio.
  • Set separate goals for short-term spending and long-term growth.
  • Revisit your budget after income changes, major expenses or changes in interest rates.
  • For complex debt or hardship, consider a reputable nonprofit credit counselor or qualified financial professional.
